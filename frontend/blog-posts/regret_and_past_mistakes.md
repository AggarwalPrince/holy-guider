Regret is one of the heaviest things a human being can carry. It traps us in a time machine, forcing us to replay a moment we can no longer change, wishing we had been wiser, kinder, or braver. 

While every spiritual tradition values accountability, none of them want you to spend the rest of your life chained to your past. They all offer a doorway out of regret and a chance to start over.

## Hinduism: the present shapes the future

It is easy to misunderstand *karma* as a permanent punishment for past mistakes. But karma simply means "action." While you may have to navigate the consequences of a past choice, your current actions are what shape your future. The Bhagavad Gita emphasizes that no matter what your past looks like, stepping into right action (*dharma*) right now immediately changes your spiritual trajectory. 

**Try today:** Look at a mistake you've been dwelling on, and ask yourself: *What is the most honorable way to act in the aftermath of it today?*

## Christianity: the old has gone, the new is here

The Christian concept of grace is built entirely around the idea that your past does not have the final word on your identity. The New Testament writers, many of whom had deeply flawed pasts, write that anyone who turns to God becomes a "new creation." The old is gone; the new is here. You are not defined by the worst thing you have ever done. 

If this regret involves someone who hurt you, you might also find peace in [How to Forgive: Wisdom from Four Religions](/blog/scripture-on-forgiveness-across-religions/).

**Try today:** Write down the thing you regret on a piece of paper, read it one last time, and then physically tear it up and throw it away as a symbol of release.

## Buddhism: the you who made the mistake is gone

A core teaching in Buddhism is impermanence (*anicca*)—the idea that everything, including you, is constantly changing. The person you were a year ago, or even yesterday, when you made that mistake, literally no longer exists. You have new knowledge and a new mind today. You can drop the baggage of the past because there is no fixed, permanent "self" that must be punished forever. You are allowed to begin again.

**Try today:** Say quietly to yourself: *I did what I did with the understanding I had then. I have a different understanding now.*

## Islam: turning back to mercy

In Islam, despairing over your past is heavily discouraged. The door of *Tawbah* (turning back in repentance) is always open. The Quran repeatedly refers to God as the Most Forgiving, the Most Merciful, and explicitly says, "Do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins" (39:53). The mistake was a moment of forgetfulness; the return is a moment of elevation. 

**Try today:** If your mistake requires an apology to someone, make the intention to deliver it plainly today. If not, make a quiet prayer asking to finally lay the guilt down.
