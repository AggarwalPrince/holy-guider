Loneliness is not the same as being alone. You can be surrounded by people and still feel unseen. Spiritual traditions have thought about this for thousands of years, and they tend to offer two kinds of comfort: a sense of being held by something larger, and a push toward real human connection.

## Hinduism: the same presence in everything

In chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna describes the person who sees the divine in all beings and all beings in the divine, and says that such a person is never lost to the divine, nor the divine to them. The idea is that separation is partly an illusion, and a quiet practice of attention can reveal connection.

See also [7 Bhagavad Gita Teachings for Anxiety and Overthinking](/blog/bhagavad-gita-quotes-for-anxiety/).

**Try today:** Spend five minutes in silence or in a natural setting, and notice that you are part of what is around you.

## Christianity: you are not forgotten

The Psalms speak often to isolation. Psalm 68 says God sets the lonely in families, and Psalm 23 speaks of walking through the darkest valley without fear because God is present. Christian life also emphasizes community, meaning the church as a body rather than a solo journey.

**Try today:** Read Psalm 23 slowly, then send one message to someone you haven't spoken to in a while.

## Buddhism: connection through compassion

Buddhist teaching emphasizes the *sangha*, the community of practitioners, as one of the three jewels. Loving-kindness meditation is also a direct antidote to isolation. Wishing others well, even strangers, tends to shift the feeling of being cut off.

**Try today:** Sit for five minutes and silently wish well to three people: someone close, someone neutral, and yourself.

## Islam: God is closer than you think

The Quran says that God is closer to us than our jugular vein (50:16), and that when a servant asks about Him, He is near and responds to those who call (2:186). Islam also stresses community through congregational prayer, the Friday gathering and caring for neighbors.

**Try today:** Make a short dua in your own words, talking to God the way you would to a trusted friend.

## Sikhism and beyond

Sikh teaching places great weight on *sangat*, the holy fellowship, and on remembering the Divine through *naam simran*. Many other traditions pair inner practice with outer community in the same way.
