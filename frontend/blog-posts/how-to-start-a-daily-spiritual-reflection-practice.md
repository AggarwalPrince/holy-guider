Most people who want a daily reflection practice never start one — not because they don't care, but because the version they picture (twenty minutes of silent meditation at 6am) doesn't survive contact with a real week. Here's a version that does.

## Start with a question, not a technique

You don't need to learn a meditation method to reflect well. You just need one honest question, asked at the same time each day:

- *What am I carrying right now that I haven't looked at directly?*
- *What did I avoid today, and why?*
- *Where did I feel most like myself today — and least?*

Pick one. Sit with it for five minutes. That's the whole practice.

## Anchor it to something that already happens

New habits fail when they need their own slot in the day. Attach the practice to something you already do without fail — right after you make coffee, right before you put your phone on the charger, right after you brush your teeth. The habit borrows the reliability of the anchor.

## Write, even badly

You don't need beautiful journaling. A single scrawled sentence — "felt resentful about the meeting and didn't say anything" — is more useful than a paragraph you spent ten minutes trying to phrase well. The goal is contact with what's actually true, not good writing.

## Let a tradition hold the question, if you want one

If a blank page feels too open-ended, borrowing structure from a spiritual tradition can help — a specific teaching to sit with, rather than starting from nothing. This is where scripture across traditions has always functioned less like a rulebook and more like a mirror: the Bhagavad Gita on detachment from outcomes, the Bible's Psalms on lament and honesty before God, the Dhammapada on the mind's habits, the Quran on patience through hardship. You don't need to be a scholar of any of them to sit with a single teaching for five minutes.

## Expect to miss days

The habit that survives isn't the one done perfectly — it's the one you return to without making a big story out of missing it. Miss a day, then just do it the next day. That's the entire skill.
